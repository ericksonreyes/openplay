---
name: plain-language
description: Write project documents so a 12- or 13-year-old can understand them without losing important meaning.
---

Use this skill when writing or reviewing project documents, instructions, examples, and agent skills.

## Writing rules

- Aim for a 7th-grade reader. Use common words and short sentences.
- Put one main idea in each sentence and one topic in each paragraph.
- Use active voice. Say who does what.
- Explain a technical word the first time it appears. Keep the word if the project needs it.
- Use a simple example when a rule is hard to picture.
- Use lists or tables to make steps and choices easy to scan.
- Write headings that tell readers what they will find.

## Keep the meaning correct

- Do not remove safety rules, limits, warnings, or steps to get approval.
- Keep names, commands, file paths, and code examples exact.
- Do not replace a precise rule with a vague one just to make it shorter.
- Mark examples as examples. Do not make up facts, owners, approvals, or results.
- If a simpler wording could change the meaning, keep the exact term and explain it.

## Review

- Read the text aloud. Rewrite parts that are hard to say or understand.
- Check that a new reader can tell what to do, who is responsible, and what happens next.
- Check all links and examples after editing.
